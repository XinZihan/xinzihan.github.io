Quiet Power in Library Systems

Organizing knowledge through selection and description

Collection development determines which materials enter a library. Classification places those materials within a conceptual structure. Subject headings provide approved terms for discovery. Metadata connects records to names, topics, formats, and communities.

These activities are necessary. A catalogue cannot function without some form of organization. Yet organization is never purely technical. Drabinski (2013) argues that classification systems should be understood as ideological texts rather than neutral containers. Categories are created within particular historical conditions. They reflect the language available to cataloguers and the institutions that authorize that language. No final correction can make a classification system permanently neutral.

The controversy surrounding the Library of Congress subject heading “Illegal Aliens” provides a clear example. Lo (2019) documents how a subject term can carry political meaning while appearing to be a routine cataloguing decision. Users who reject an official term may still need to search with it because records were created under that heading. The catalogue places a burden on the user: understand the system’s language or risk missing relevant material.

Bias can also appear in classification structure. Howard and Knowlton (2018) found problems in Library of Congress systems used for African American Studies and LGBTQIA Studies. Materials may be dispersed across categories or placed under language that does not reflect current community understanding. Browsing a shelf can therefore reproduce the assumptions built into the classification scheme.

These examples show why library power can be quiet.

A library does not need to ban a book to limit its discoverability. The material may be present but described poorly. It may be placed where users do not expect to find it. Its subject language may represent an outsider’s perspective. The result is a form of partial access.

Realize it, respond to it
