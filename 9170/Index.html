Information Literacy Guide for you

Search results may look neutral, but they are shaped by algorithms, classification systems, metadata, popularity, and institutional decisions. These systems influence which information you see first and which knowledge remains difficult to find.

This website explores how libraries can help people search beyond dominant information systems, especially when knowledge produced by marginalized communities is overlooked or ranked below more visible sources.

How to choose your news? Here is the guide

You may not be able to avoid every algorithm, but you can learn how search systems shape visibility and create more than one pathway to information.

How Can Libraries Help Marginalized Communities Search Beyond Algorithmic Systems?

Information may exist without being easy to discover. A source can be hidden by unfamiliar terminology, outdated subject headings, low search rankings, limited digitization, or a lack of institutional attention.

You may also find that large organizations appear before smaller community groups, even when local sources provide more relevant knowledge.

Why This Project Matters

The digital divide is not only about access to devices or the internet. It is also about whether you can find information that reflects your identity, language, experiences, and community.

Search engines and library systems organize large amounts of information, but their categories and rankings are not complete or neutral. You may need to compare different systems, change your search language, and follow community-created pathways to find a wider range of perspectives.
